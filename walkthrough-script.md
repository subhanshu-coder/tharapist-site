# Five-minute client walkthrough: Dr. Maya Reynolds website draft

**Recording guide:** Use Loom to record the browser at desktop width. Around 2:30, switch to a narrow mobile preview and scroll through the page. Speak naturally; this is a first draft demo, so invite feedback at the end.

## 0:00–0:35 — Welcome

Hi Dr. Reynolds. Here’s the first draft of your new website. I’ve shaped it around the information in your profile and the kinds of concerns you described working with: anxiety, panic, trauma, burnout, and perfectionism. The page is designed to feel calm and welcoming, while helping a new visitor understand quickly whether your practice might be right for them.

## 0:35–1:10 — Visual direction and hero

At the top, the colors move away from the original site’s look into deep sage, warm ivory, and a small amount of clay. The idea is to feel grounded and human without looking clinical. The headline tells visitors what you offer and where you practice, which makes the page easier to understand and easier to find locally. The short introduction speaks to the experience in your profile of seeming capable on the outside while feeling overwhelmed inside. The two buttons give visitors an obvious next step or a way to learn more before reaching out.

## 1:10–1:45 — About and services

As we move down, the introduction puts your voice and your background forward: you’re a licensed clinical psychologist in Santa Monica, and you offer support that makes space for both practical tools and deeper reflection. The services are organized around anxiety and panic, trauma, and burnout and perfectionism. Those three areas come directly from your profile, with descriptions written in plain language so visitors can quickly recognize what they may be experiencing.

## 1:45–2:20 — Approach

This section explains how you work. It names CBT, EMDR, mindfulness, and body-oriented techniques, and reinforces that treatment is collaborative and tailored to each person. I’ve also made the pacing and focus on safety especially visible in the trauma language, since your profile describes stabilization and feeling more regulated as important parts of that work.

## 2:20–2:45 — Consultation invitation

Here’s the invitation to take a first step. The copy lets visitors know that sessions are available in person in Santa Monica or by secure telehealth for people located in California. The contact button is a placeholder for now; it can be connected to your preferred contact or scheduling link once you choose one.

## 2:45–3:25 — Our office

This is the new office section requested for the assignment. It uses the office images from your profile and describes the space as quiet, private, comfortable, uncluttered, and naturally lit, following the details you provided. The address shown is copied from the document. Before launch, I’d like to confirm that the street name and number are correct, because the document spells the street as “123th Street 45 W.”

## 3:25–4:00 — FAQs

The FAQ section answers common questions about a first appointment, in-person and online sessions, your therapy methods, and who you work with. It offers useful information without making promises about outcomes or adding details that weren’t in your profile.

## 4:00–4:45 — Mobile walkthrough

Now I’m switching to the mobile view. The navigation becomes a simple menu link, the sections stack into one readable column, and the service cards and office images resize for a narrow screen. The main consultation button remains easy to find, and the questions can be opened one at a time so the page doesn’t feel overwhelming on a phone.

## 4:45–5:00 — Close

That’s the first draft. It’s designed to help people understand your approach, feel welcomed, and see a clear next step. Once the address and consultation link are confirmed, I can replace the placeholders and refine any wording or visual details based on your feedback. Thank you for taking a look.
